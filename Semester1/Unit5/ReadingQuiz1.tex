\section{Forces II}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A $5.00\times 10^5$ kg rocket is accelerating straight up. Its engines produce $1.250\times 10^7$ N of thrust, and air resistance is $4.50\times 10^6$ N. What is the rocket's acceleration? \\ \vspace{1.5cm}
\item Two children pull a third child on a snow saucer sled exerting forces $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ as shown from above in Fig. \ref{fig:1}.  Find the acceleration of the 50.00-kg sled and child system. Note that the direction of the frictional force will be in the opposite direction of $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2$. \\ \vspace{4cm}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Forces III}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose the children let go of the sled in the previous exercise, and the sled crosses onto a large patch of ice.  (a) If the total mass of the rider plus the sled is 50 kg, what is the \textit{weight} of the system? What is the normal force? (b) The force of friction is $\vec{f} = -\mu N\hat{i}$, where $\mu$ is the coefficient of friction and $N$ is the magnitude of the normal force.  If $\mu$ for sled and ice is 0.02, what is the force of friction? (c) When will the sled stop, if the net force is solely friction? \\ \vspace{3cm}
\item Sometimes, friction can be \textit{static}.  That is, there's enough friction to keep an object stuck in position.  Suppose a sled is on a hill with incline angle $\theta$, and the coefficient of static friction is $\mu_s$.  Show that the maximum $\theta$, such that the sled does not move, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mu_k = \tan\theta
\end{equation}
\vspace{2cm}
\item The force of \textit{drag} (Memory Bank) on a diving falcon sets its maximum speed.  Show that, if a falcon is diving straight down to catch prey, that the fastest speed at which it can travel is
\begin{equation}
v = \sqrt{\frac{2mg}{C\rho A}}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the area of the falcon, $\rho$ is the density of air, $m$ is the mass of the falcon, and $g = 9.81$ m s$^{-2}$.  Let the constant $C = 1$.  Estimate the maximum speed of a falcon.
\end{enumerate}
