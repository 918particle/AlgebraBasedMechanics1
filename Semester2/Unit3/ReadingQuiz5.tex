\section{Memory Bank}

\begin{itemize}
\item $V_{\rm H} = B l v$ ... The Hall voltage, given external B-field, height of conductor, $l$, and the drift velocity $v$.
\item $\vec{F} = I\vec{L} \times \vec{B}$ ... The Lorentz force on a current.
\item $\vec{F} = I \vec{L} \times \vec{B}$ ... Lorentz Force on a Current
\item $\vec{F} = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ ... Lorentz Force on a Charge
\item $\vec{B} = (\mu_0 I)/(2\pi r)\hat{\phi}$ ... The magnetic field $B$ \textit{caused} by the current $I$ a distance $r$ away.  The field \textit{circles} the wire, so the direction of $\vec{B}$ is $\hat{\phi}$.
\item $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T m A$^{-1}$.
\item $\vec{B} = \mu_0 n I \hat{z}$ ... The B-field inside a solenoid.  $n=N/L$, the turns per unit length.
\end{itemize}

\section{Amp\`{e}re's Law}

\begin{enumerate}
\item (a) What is the B-field strength 0.75 cm away from a wire carrying 1.33 A of current? (b) Describe the shape or direction of this field. \\ \vspace{1.5cm}
\item Consider the \textit{tokamak} in Fig. \ref{fig:para}.  Using the right-hand rule, convince yourself that the external current creates a $B$-field inside the toroid that runs along the circumference of the toroid.  If there are charged particles moving around the ring, trapped by the toroidal field, use the right-hand rule again to convince yourself these charged particles create a \textit{poloidal} field.
\item Inside a \textit{solenoid} (a linear toroid), the B-field is constant, $\mu_0 n I$, where $n$ is the number of wire coils per unit length.  (a) If we measure B to be 150 gauss inside a solenoid running 0.5 A of current, what will the B-field be at 5 A? (b) If we cut the solenoid in half, so that it is half as long, what will the B-field be at 5 A?
\end{enumerate}
